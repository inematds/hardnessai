5.1~30 min

🧹 The 2-step reset

Go back to zero: delete everything, observe the pure agent, and add back only what’s missing.

What it is:

Reset the setup to a blank slate to see what really matters.

Why learn:

Setups accumulate bloat; a reset reveals what remains.

Key concepts:

Zero state; eliminate accumulation.

What it is:

Remove skills, instructions, and configs until only the raw agent remains.

Why learn:

It’s the step that gives you the courage to really cut back.

Key concepts:

Full wipe; back up first.

What it is:

Run tasks with the agent without any harness and see where it stumbles.

Why learn:

Only this way will you know what you actually need to add.

Key concepts:

Raw baseline; observe the gaps.

What it is:

Add back only procedures and skills that address real gaps.

Why learn:

Each piece comes back justified, not out of habit.

Key concepts:

Intentional layers; one at a time.

What it is:

Resist the temptation to put everything back; reintroduce only what’s strictly useful.

Why learn:

Keep context light and performance high.

Key concepts:

Minimum viable harness.

What it is:

Use reset as a sandbox to test a new configuration.

Why learn:

Lets you experiment without worrying about breaking the main setup.

Key concepts:

Reset as a lab; rapid iteration.

🔍 grill-me + 10 decisions

Align before coding: the grill-me skill and the 10-decisions prompt, ready to paste.

What it is:

The complete text of the grill-me skill, ready to save in your setup.

Why learn:

It’s the most reusable artifact in the entire course.

Key concepts:

Short skill (4-5 sentences); adversarial interview.

What it is:

A prompt that makes the agent list the 10 most important decisions in the work.

Why learn:

Focuses the interview on what actually drives the result.

Key concepts:

Top 10 decisions; explicit prioritization.

What it is:

Ask the agent to interview you until it reaches ~98% understanding.

Why learn:

Creates shared understanding before a single line of code.

Key concepts:

Trust goal; align first.

What it is:

Invoke grill-me at the start of any ambiguous or large task.

Why learn:

Replaces plan mode and avoids rework.

Key concepts:

Before coding; ambiguous tasks.

What it is:

Tailor the skill to your project's domain, tone, and constraints.

Why learn:

A custom grill-me asks what matters most to you.

Key concepts:

Templating; domain context.

What it is:

A skill that's too long, vague questions, or skipping the interview because you're in a hurry.

Why learn:

Keeps grill-me from becoming just another ritual with no effect.

Key concepts:

Short and specific; don't skip alignment.
